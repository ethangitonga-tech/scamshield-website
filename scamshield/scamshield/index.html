Watch: how scammers trick people

A 21-second explainer of the four steps almost every scam follows.

Read the video transcript
  1. Contact: a call, SMS, email or message from someone you don't know.
  2. Pressure: "Act now!" Fear, urgency or a prize makes you rush.
  3. Payment: they ask for your PIN, a code, or money sent to a stranger.
  4. Vanish: the scammer disappears and the money is gone.
  5. Your defence: Stop. Verify. Report.

Can you spot the red flags?

This message looks harmless at first glance. The highlighted parts are warning signs.

Example: a fake "account blocked" SMS

From: unknown number Dear customer, your account will be blocked within 24 hours. Click here to verify now: secure-login-update.example and enter your PIN.
  • Threat and deadline to make you panic
  • Link that is not the official website
  • Asks for your PIN, which real providers never do
The safe response
  1. Do not click the link or reply.
  2. Open the official app or call the number on your card or the official website.
  3. Forward or report the message to your provider, then delete it.

Quick answers

What is the number one rule to avoid scams?
Never act under pressure. Real banks, mobile networks and employers will not mind if you stop and verify first.
Will a real company ever ask for my PIN or password?
No. Banks, mobile money providers and reputable websites never ask for your PIN, password or one-time code by SMS, call or chat.
I already sent money. What now?
Contact your bank or mobile money provider immediately, then report to the police. The step-by-step guide shows exactly what to do.
