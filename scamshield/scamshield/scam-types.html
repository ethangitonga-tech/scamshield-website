Scam types explained

How each scam works, what it looks like, and the warning signs to watch for.

Phishing Mobile money Romance Investment Jobs, shops, prizes, impersonation Comparison table Example messages
1. PHISHING

Fake emails, links and websites

The scammer pretends to be your bank, mobile network, delivery company or employer. The message asks you to click a link and "verify" your account, then the fake page captures what you type.

  • Urgent threats such as "Your account will be closed"
  • Spelling mistakes and strange sender addresses
  • Links that do not match the real website
2. MOBILE MONEY

Fake "sent by mistake" and reversal calls

You receive a fake confirmation SMS, then a call asking you to send the money back. Others pose as customer care and ask for your PIN to "fix" a problem.

  • Always check your real balance, not just the SMS
  • Never share your PIN or one-time codes with anyone
  • Genuine reversals go through your provider, not a stranger
3. ROMANCE

Love that ends in a money request

A "perfect" stranger on social media or a dating app chats for weeks. Then comes a sudden hospital bill, travel problem or business emergency.

  • They refuse video calls or meeting in person
  • Their story keeps changing
  • They ask for money, gift cards or crypto
4. INVESTMENT

"Guaranteed" profits and pyramid schemes

Fake trading platforms, crypto "doublers" and pyramid schemes show fake profits to make you invest more, then block withdrawals.

  • Promises of high returns with no risk
  • Pressure to recruit friends and family
  • No clear licence or regulator you can verify

More scams to know

These four are just as common and just as costly.

5. FAKE JOBS

Pay first, no job later

You are asked for a "registration", "training" or "visa" fee for a job that does not exist. Real employers do not charge you to hire you.

6. FAKE SHOPS

Too cheap to be true

Brand-new items at 90% off, but nothing arrives. Check reviews, the seller's history, and pay on delivery where possible.

7. PRIZE SCAMS

"You won!" (you never entered)

You must pay a "tax" or "delivery fee" to claim a prize. A real prize never requires you to pay first.

8. IMPERSONATION

Not who they say they are

Someone pretends to be police, your bank, a relative or your boss. Hang up and call back using a number you trust.

Example messages and their red flags

These are made-up examples. Real scams look just like them.

"Sent by mistake" message

From: unknown number Hello, I sent KSh 5,000 to your number by mistake. Please return it urgently, I need it for hospital.
  • Emotional story to rush you
  • Check your own balance before you do anything

Fake job offer

From: "HR Manager" Congratulations! You are selected for the job. Pay a refundable fee of KSh 1,500 today to confirm your slot.
  • You never applied or interviewed
  • Real employers never charge to hire

Investment pitch

From: unknown group Invest KSh 10,000 and get KSh 20,000 in 7 days. 100% guaranteed. Only 5 slots left!
  • No investment is guaranteed
  • Fake scarcity creates panic
Remember: the exact words change, but the pattern is the same: urgency, secrecy, and a request for money or codes.
