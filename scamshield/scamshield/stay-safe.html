How to protect yourself

Simple habits that stop most scams, and what to do if you have been targeted.

Red flags Safety checklist Secure your accounts If you were scammed Where to report FAQ

Red flags

If you notice any of these, pause immediately.

  • Pressure to act "right now"
  • Request for your PIN, password or one-time code
  • Payment demanded by gift card, crypto or mobile money to a stranger
  • Offers that sound too good to be true
  • Unknown numbers claiming to be family or officials
  • Links that look slightly different from the real website
  • "Keep this secret" or "do not tell anyone"
  • You are asked to pay to receive a job, loan or prize

Stay safe checklist

Build these habits and most scams fail.

  • Stop: take a breath before you reply or click
  • Verify by calling the company on its official number
  • Check the web address carefully before entering details
  • Talk to a trusted person before sending money
  • Keep your phone and apps updated
  • Do not open attachments or links from strangers
  • Use only official app stores and websites
  • Report suspicious messages, then delete them

Secure your accounts

Even if a scammer tricks you once, strong settings limit the damage.

PASSWORDS

Unique and long

Use a different password for every important account. A password manager makes this easy.

2-STEP LOGIN

Turn it on

Enable two-step verification on email, social media and banking, and never share the codes.

PHONE & PIN

Lock your phone

Use a screen lock, keep your mobile money PIN private, and change it if you suspect it is known.

PRIVACY

Share less

Limit personal details on social media. Scammers use them to sound convincing.

Already scammed? Act fast

Speed matters. Work through these steps in order.

  1. Stop all contact with the scammer. Do not send "one last payment" to recover your money.
  2. Call your bank or mobile money provider immediately to report the transaction and ask what can be blocked or reversed.
  3. Change your passwords and PINs, starting with email, banking and mobile money.
  4. Keep evidence: screenshots, phone numbers, transaction messages and receipts.
  5. Report to the police and to your mobile network or platform.
  6. Warn family and friends so the same scammer cannot reach them.
Remember: being scammed is not your fault. Scammers are professionals. Reporting quickly helps you and protects others.

Where to report

Use only official channels you find on the organisation's own website, app or card.

Your bank or provider

Report the transaction first. They can freeze cards, block accounts and flag the recipient.

Your mobile network

Many networks let you forward scam SMS to a free short code (for example, Safaricom customers can forward them to 333). Check your own provider's official page.

The police

File a report at your nearest police station or through official online channels. Bring your evidence.

The platform

Report the profile, ad or page on Facebook, Instagram, WhatsApp, TikTok or the marketplace where it appeared.

Help the people around you

Older relatives, new phone users and young job seekers are common targets. Share the red flags above, agree on a family "safe word" for emergencies, and tell them they can always call you before sending money.

Frequently asked questions

Can I get my money back?
Sometimes. The faster you report to your bank or provider, the better the chance that a transfer can be flagged or blocked. There are no guarantees, which is why prevention matters most.
How do I know a website is real?
Type the address yourself or use the official app. Check the spelling of the domain, look for the padlock, and remember that a padlock alone does not prove a site is honest.
A caller knows my name and details. Is it real?
Not necessarily. Scammers buy or collect personal details. Hang up and call the organisation back on its official number.
Is it safe to click "unsubscribe" or "stop" in a suspicious SMS?
It is safer to ignore and delete it, and report it to your provider. Replying can confirm that your number is active.
